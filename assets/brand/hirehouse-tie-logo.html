HireHouse — Tie Identity

The lowercase i is a tittle over a stem. A necktie is a knot over a blade. The anatomy already matches, so the tie replaces the letter instead of sitting on top of it — and the word still reads as HireHouse at a glance. 13 assets, light and dark, all outlined vector with no font dependency.

Two tie drawings, on purpose. The wordmark tie is set to the x-height (1080 units), so it sits level with the i, r, e, o, u and s instead of towering over them, and the word keeps an even colour. The mark tie is stubbier, drawn straight into a 100 field, because a slim tie at 16px is a smudge. Check the size ladder on 03 Tie mark: that is the shape doing the work in a browser tab.

The letter slot opens. The i is 404 units wide; the tie needs 540. That extra space is deliberate and is part of the mark — do not close it up, and do not re-set the wordmark in live text and paste a tie over it. Use the files.

Ink is not black. The letters sit on #1F2430, a near-black with a blue cast, so the tie reads as a colour event rather than a clash. On dark grounds the letters go pure white.

One colour event. 01 Primary keeps the whole word in ink and lets the tie carry the only accent — that is the version I would ship. 02 Split keeps continuity with the deck's Hire/House colour break, but you get three colour events competing and the tie stops being the thing you notice first.

Never. Put the tie on any letter but the i or I. Outline it, add a gradient, or add a dimple to the knot. Recolour the letters to the accent. Set the tie in a colour outside the palette — if you want the reference's red, that is a palette decision for the whole brand, not a logo decision.
